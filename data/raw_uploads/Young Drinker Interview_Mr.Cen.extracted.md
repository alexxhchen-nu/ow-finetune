**Interview Summary**

**The discussion focused on drinking habits, consumption occasions,
brand preferences, purchase channels, and the suitability of whisky,
baijiu, and wine in China. Key takeaways are as follows:**

- **Overall Drinking Habits and Frequency**

  - Drinking frequency is relatively high overall, with openness to
    almost all types of alcohol.

  - When drinking alone, the main choices are beer and whisky; when
    drinking with family or friends, the choice usually follows the
    group's preference.

  - Beer is consumed more frequently, roughly every week, mainly because
    it is easy to buy.

  - Whisky is consumed less often, at around once or twice per month.

  - In family and friend gathering occasions, the most common choices
    are beer and baijiu, with wine consumed occasionally as well.

- **Key Whisky Consumption Occasions**

  - Whisky mainly appears in two scenarios:

    - At bars, usually as a small serve such as a 50ml pour;

    - At home, by keeping a full bottle and drinking a little at a time.

  - In bar occasions, cocktail bars or lounge bars are more common when
    going with friends, while whisky bars are more likely when going
    alone.

  - Whisky is clearly more personal preference-driven than socially
    driven.

  - Emotional state is not a decisive trigger: whisky may be consumed
    when feeling happy, unhappy, or occasionally with a girlfriend.

- **Whisky Entry Path and Preference Formation**

  - The initial entry point into whisky came from buying base spirits
    and making cocktails at home.

  - Early purchases were typically priced at around RMB 100--200 per
    bottle, including whisky, rum, and gin.

  - After trying multiple spirits, whisky gradually stood out because it
    was seen as having:

    - A larger market presence;

    - Greater flavour diversity across products.

  - Compared with other base spirits, whisky was preferred both for its
    taste and for its broader room for flavour exploration.

  - There is also a clear tendency to try unfamiliar flavours in bars
    and, if the experience is positive, to later purchase a full bottle
    for home consumption.

- **Whisky Selection Criteria and Brand Perception**

  - Brand itself is not the primary selection factor; flavour matters
    more.

  - Recent preference leans toward floral and fruity styles.

  - Brands mentioned included:

    - Glenfiddich

    - Yamazaki

    - Macallan 12 / 18

    - Johnnie Walker

    - Highland Park

  - Single malt versus blended is not a major decision criterion.

  - Highland Park was viewed more positively because:

    - It offers a broader range of options;

    - It covers a wider price range;

    - Product-to-product flavour differences are more noticeable.

  - Macallan was seen as:

    - Less differentiated across expressions;

    - More premium-positioned and therefore more expensive, leading to
      lower ordering frequency.

- **Consumption Occasions for Baijiu and Wine**

  - Both baijiu and wine are much more closely tied to shared meal and
    group occasions than whisky.

  - Typical baijiu occasions include:

    - Meals with parents;

    - Family gatherings with relatives and family friends;

    - Small gatherings with two or three close friends.

  - Wine appears in similar situations, such as:

    - Family meals with many relatives;

    - Meals with parents and their friends;

    - Opening a bottle at home during dinner with parents.

  - In both baijiu and wine occasions, product choice is usually driven
    by the setting and by others, rather than by active personal
    selection.

  - Both baijiu and wine are consumed less frequently than whisky, with
    frequency mainly depending on how often gatherings take place.

- **Core Differences Between Whisky, Baijiu, and Wine**

  - Whisky is primarily a matter of personal preference and individual
    consumption.

  - Baijiu is much more occasion-led and socially embedded, especially
    in meal-based group settings.

  - Wine, similar to baijiu, is also more dependent on group dining
    occasions than on solo drinking.

  - Baijiu is more commonly chosen over whisky in friend gatherings
    because friends are generally more comfortable with baijiu and less
    accustomed to whisky.

  - Even when whisky has been introduced to friends, baijiu remains the
    preferred common choice.

  - There is limited familiarity with baijiu brands and pricing, mainly
    because baijiu choices are usually made by others, and there is no
    clear explanation for why some younger friends prefer baijiu.

- **Fit of Whisky in Chinese Consumption Occasions**

  - Whisky is not entirely without potential in meal occasions, friend
    gatherings, or even business-related settings, but its overall fit
    remains weaker than baijiu.

  - One key reason is that many Chinese consumers have not necessarily
    grown to like baijiu naturally; rather, they have adapted to it
    through repeated social exposure over time.

  - This long-term "social adaptation" gives baijiu a structural
    advantage at the Chinese dining table.

  - If baijiu were replaced by whisky, many consumers would find whisky
    harder to accept.

  - On the one hand, people who do not naturally enjoy drinking would
    need to adapt to another alcohol type and another aroma profile; on
    the other hand, people already used to baijiu may have little
    incentive to switch.

  - As a result, whisky remains more common in personal occasions than
    in group meal occasions in China.

  - That said, broader acceptance may still be possible if more
    consumers are given a chance to try whisky for the first time.

- **Whisky and Food Pairing**

  - Whisky is generally not consumed during formal meals.

  - It is seen as more suitable with snacks or light bites rather than
    full meal occasions.

  - This appears to be more a matter of personal habit than a definitive
    statement that whisky cannot pair with food.

  - When eating alone, beer is still the more likely pairing choice.

- **Purchase Channels and View on Instant Retail**

  - Whisky is mainly purchased through online platforms, especially
    Taobao and JD.com.

  - Initial purchases were already in full-size bottles (500ml / 750ml)
    rather than small convenience-store formats.

  - Confidence in buying full bottles comes partly from having tried the
    product previously in bars or other out-of-home occasions.

  - Instant delivery channels such as Ele.me and Meituan are not
    preferred, mainly because:

    - They are perceived as more expensive;

    - There is no strong "must-have-it-now" urgency when buying whisky.

- **Post-Meeting Reflections and Implications**

  - The profile was viewed as relatively typical of current whisky
    consumption patterns:

    - Whisky is highly personal and individual in nature;

    - Once consumption becomes social, cocktails, baijiu, or other more
      group-friendly alcohol types tend to take over.

  - The idea of "drinking whisky with meals" was seen as relatively
    uncommon, highlighting the difficulty of educating the Chinese
    dining-table occasion for whisky.

  - Similar challenges were noted not only for whisky but also for
    Cognac.

  - Hennessy, for example, has been pushing the "Discovering Chinese
    Flavors" initiative since 2015, yet has still not fully succeeded in
    embedding such products into everyday Chinese dining culture.

  - Overall, whisky at this stage is better described as a
    personal-interest category rather than a mature group dining
    category.
