**Interview Summary**

**The discussion focused on overall drinking habits, whisky consumption
occasions, brand perceptions, purchase channels, and barriers to broader
adoption. Key takeaways are as follows:**

- **Overall Drinking Habits and Frequency**

  - Drinking frequency was historically high, at times reaching daily
    consumption or at least three times per week

  - Current drinking frequency has declined significantly after surgery,
    especially for at-home consumption

  - Category exposure is broad, covering wine, whisky, baijiu, gin, rum,
    tequila, vodka, brandy, and champagne

  - In terms of overall frequency, consumption appears to be led by:

    - White wine and sweeter low-alcohol wines

    - Red wine

    - Whisky

- **Core Consumption Occasions by Category**

  - Wine is most closely associated with meal occasions and home
    consumption

  - Typical pairings include:

    - Red wine with red meat or braised dishes

    - White wine with seafood or summer meals

    - Champagne for celebratory moments

  - Whisky is more concentrated in bar occasions, post-dinner occasions,
    and small-group social settings

  - Whisky is rarely consumed in restaurants; if drinking continues
    after dinner, the occasion typically shifts to a bar

  - Solo drinking is limited, with alcohol consumption more often taking
    place with friends or family

- **Key Whisky Consumption Patterns**

  - Whisky is usually consumed neat or on the rocks, with a personal
    preference for ice

  - In bars, whisky may be consumed directly or through whisky-based
    cocktails, especially Whiskey Sour

  - Occasion size influences the choice of alcohol:

    - For two people, wine is more common

    - For four or more strong drinkers, ordering a full bottle of whisky
      becomes more likely

  - In stronger drinking groups, whisky can be part of multi-bottle
    sessions alongside other categories

- **Whisky Entry Path and Preference Formation**

  - Entry into whisky came through two main routes:

    - Cocktail-led exposure, particularly through Whiskey Sour

    - Family-led exposure in Australia, where whisky was introduced in a
      home setting

  - Broader interest in whisky was strengthened by exposure to Western
    drinking culture

  - Deeper engagement is linked not only to taste, but also to interest
    in whisky knowledge, including aging, fermentation, distillation,
    and cask characteristics

- **Brand Preferences and Purchase Channels**

  - The first whisky brands that come to mind are Macallan and Hibiki,
    with Glenfiddich also mentioned

  - Macallan is perceived as a premium brand, although pricing is
    understood mainly through bar menus and age statements

  - Japanese whisky appears relatively common in bar ordering

  - Home whisky is mainly sourced through:

    - Overseas purchases brought back from trips

    - Employee internal sales at lower prices

    - Online or instant retail purchases when needed for gifting or
      immediate use

- Views on Other Western Spirits

  - Brandy is mainly associated with KTV occasions rather than everyday
    personal drinking; In KTV settings, consumption is often driven by
    pre-packaged offers such as:

    - XO packages

    - Hennessy set menus

  - Brandy appears to be channel-led and promotion-led, rather than
    preference-led

  - Tequila is primarily associated with shots and nightlife occasions;
    Tequila is also recognized in cocktail formats such as:

    - Tequila Sunrise

    - Tequila Sunset

  - Overall, compared with whisky, brandy and tequila are seen as more
    occasion-specific and less central to regular drinking habits.

- **Barriers to Broader Whisky Adoption**

  - The main barriers are high alcohol strength and low
    beginner-friendliness

  - Whisky is seen as requiring a certain level of drinking maturity
    before consumers are willing to explore it

  - Compared with cocktails, whisky is less intuitive and less
    accessible for first-time drinkers

  - Alcohol-infused products such as whisky ice cream may encourage
    trial, but are unlikely to convert non-drinkers into regular whisky
    drinkers

  - There also appears to be a gender skew, with women perceived as more
    likely to prefer red wine than whisky
