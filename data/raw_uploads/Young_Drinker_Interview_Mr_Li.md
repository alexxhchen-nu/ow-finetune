**Interview Summary**

**The discussion focused first on drinking frequency across alcohol
types, and then on whisky consumption habits, brand choices, purchase
channels, drinking occasions, and barriers to wider adoption. Key
takeaways are as follows:**

- **Overall Drinking Habits and Frequency**

  - Overall drinking frequency is relatively high

  - Based on the supplementary question:

    - Alcohol consumption can reach three to four times per week

    - When work is lighter, bar visits may be one to two times per week,
      or around three to four times per month on average

    - 50ml miniatures may be bought from convenience stores

    - Larger bottles are bought for bars or home stocking

    - Whisky is usually consumed neat, without mixers

  - Baijiu is mainly consumed in formal occasions, such as drinking with
    teachers or leaders

- **Whisky Consumption Habits**

  - Whisky is mainly consumed in bars or at home

  - The preferred way of drinking is simple:

    - Usually neat

    - At most with one ice cube or ice ball

    - Rarely with anything added

  - Whisky plays a more personal role than cocktails or other social
    drinks

- **How the Preference for Whisky Developed**

  - The initial preference was vodka, which was seen as cleaner and less
    pungent

  - The shift to whisky started during graduate school in Milan

  - Key triggers included:

    - Seeing Italian classmates drink whisky while smoking

    - Feeling that whisky had a mature and stylish image

    - Liking the smoky flavor after trying it

  - Over time, whisky became appealing both for taste and for the
    stronger drinking experience

- **Key Reasons for Liking Whisky**

  - The main attraction is the smoky flavor

  - Whisky is also associated with:

    - A more mature image

    - Stronger sensory stimulation

    - Higher alcohol content than mixed drinks

  - Higher alcohol strength is seen as creating a stronger sense of
    excitement and expression

- **Social Acceptance of Whisky**

  - Among friends, whisky has limited acceptance

  - The main reasons are:

    - High alcohol content

    - Low acceptance of drinking it neat

    - Preference for sweeter drinks

  - In group settings, friends are more likely to choose:

    - Cocktails

    - Sweeter classic drinks

    - Highballs rather than neat whisky

  - Overall, whisky is still more individual than social

- **Brand Preferences and Price Sensitivity**

  - Price is a major factor in brand choice

  - Jack Daniel's is the main regular choice because it is affordable

  - Other brands mentioned included:

    - Japanese whisky brands

    - Macallan

  - Macallan was recognized and tried, but the difference versus
    lower-priced whisky was not seen as obvious

  - Brand knowledge beyond the most visible labels is still limited

- **Purchase Channels**

  - Both miniatures and full bottles are purchased

  - Meituan Waima is now a main purchase channel

  - The reasons include:

    - Fast delivery

    - A strong sense of immediacy

    - Competitive pricing

    - Better perceived reliability than smaller merchants

  - Authenticity matters, especially given concern about counterfeit
    alcohol

  - Discounts and membership offers also support channel choice

- **Fit of Whisky by Occasion**

  - Whisky fits bar occasions much better than meal occasions

  - It feels natural in:

    - Bars

    - At-home solo drinking

    - Non-meal occasions

  - It feels less natural in

    - Chinese dining occasions

    - Group meals

    - Formal food settings

  - Even for Western meals, red wine is seen as the more standard
    choice.

- **Whisky Versus Baijiu and Wine**

  - Baijiu and wine are both more meal-driven than whisky

  - Baijiu is mostly consumed passively in formal social settings

  - Wine is more relevant in Western meal occasions, but without deep
    product involvement

  - Compared with baijiu and wine, whisky is less embedded in shared
    dining culture.

- **Whisky and Food Pairing**

  - Whisky is generally not seen as a good match with Chinese food

  - Specific examples mentioned as poor fits included:

    - Skewers

    - Hot pot

    - Jiangsu and Zhejiang cuisine

  - The overall view is that whisky works better as a standalone bar
    drink than as a meal-pairing drink.

- **Barriers to Wider Whisky Adoption**

  - Two core barriers were identified:

    - Taste threshold

    - Occasion mismatch

  - Many people find whisky difficult to accept because it is:

    - Relatively bitter

    - High in alcohol

    - Less sweet and approachable than other drinks

  - In addition, whisky does not fit naturally into many common dining
    occasions

  - Even regular drinkers may stay with the category they already prefer
    rather than switch to whisky

- **View on Alcohol-Infused Foods**

  - Whisky-flavored food products are unlikely to convert non-whisky
    drinkers into whisky drinkers; non-whisky drinkers have less
    possibilities to try these foods

  - Examples discussed included:

    - Whisky ice cream

    - Liquor-filled chocolates

  - The view was that these products have their own taste appeal, but do
    not recreate the real whisky-drinking experience.
